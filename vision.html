Choices

  • Build a language model that can reason about a person's choices and preferences. Today's decision aids hold a person as a few unknown weights — too impoverished an account of anyone. We will build a model of a person rich enough to train against, and use it to evaluate and build aids that actually help someone choose.
    • Show that today's models cannot help a person choose
      • Choose the first setting to work in, and the decisions it covers
      • Build a benchmark that measures how well a model helps a person reason about their own choices
      • Score frontier models on it, and show they do poorly
        • Gather the aids built for deciding — DELLMa, Decision Flow, Decisive — and score them alongside
        • Reproduce the ones we cannot run, or get them from their authors
      • Show they fail at helping a person choose, not only at helping them reason
    • Build a neural model of a user
      • Give it dimensions that hold steady and dimensions that move with context
      • Put known effects into it — a sunk cost, a standing aversion to price — alongside real ambivalence
      • Make adding one more dimension of a person a modular operation
        • Pick the dimensions that pay for themselves first
        • Hold the line between domain-agnostic psychology and a domain taken seriously
      • Earn the claim that the model is human-like enough to grade an aid against
    • Build the verifier that trains a model to reason about people
      • Build a vocalizer that says in English what tendencies the user model is exhibiting
        • Design each mechanism so the vocalizer can be trusted without leaning on probes
        • Let it voice its own uncertainty instead of passing the network straight through
        • Keep it general enough that it is not just a fitting of our own user model
      • Invert the vocalizer to recover an estimated utility function
        • Settle whether to invert at all, or read utilities off the model directly
      • Turn that utility function into a reward environment for behavior
      • Train a reasoning model against it, and beat every aid we benchmarked
        • Separate the error the user model caused from the error the vocalizer caused
        • Teach it to clarify what a person cannot articulate while it learns what they want
    • Land it where it counts
      • Grade every aid across the space of users rather than on one fixed benchmark
      • Choose the domain that carries the result, and win in it
      • Show with real users that our model helps them choose better
      • Write it up so the field cannot file it away as prompting a user persona
